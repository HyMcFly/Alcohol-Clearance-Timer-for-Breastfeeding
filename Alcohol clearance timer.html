The one scale: grams of alcohol per 100 mL

Bars show how much of your peak is still left. Milk is assumed to match blood alcohol (0.015 BAC means 0.015 g per 100 mL). Studies find them close, not identical. Alcohol-free beer is taken as 0.5% ABV, the usual upper limit for that label.

How this works, and how sure it is

EstablishedAlcohol passes from blood into milk and the two levels track each other closely, so when your blood alcohol is zero, your milk is too. Only time clears it.

ModelPeak BAC uses the Widmark approach with your body water estimated by the Watson formula (female) from your height and weight. Alcohol is treated as fully absorbed straight away, which slightly overstates the peak and does not change when it reaches zero.

ModelClearance is a steady rate per hour (the rates below are population ranges, not yours):

  • Fast: 0.020 BAC points per hour
  • Average: 0.015 per hour
  • Conservative: 0.010 per hour

LimitReal rates vary person to person and day to day, and the drink sizes and strengths here are typical values you can edit. Strong cocktails and generous pours are the usual surprise. Being under a legal limit does not mean you are unimpaired.
